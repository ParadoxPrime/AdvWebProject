Copyright, Creative Common (CC), Fair Use
New Zealand Copyright is defined in the Copyright Act 1994
The principles of NZ copyright are as follows:
  • Automatic Protection: Anything you make is automatically protected, no application required.
  • Protect Expression, not Ideas: An idea can't be copyrighted, but a tangible expression of the idea can.
  • Origin: A work must be original and made by a Kiwi to be protected by NZ law.
  • Ownership: The creator of a work is the owner, unless the work was created for an employer, such as a concept artist painting for a film studio. Additionally, commissioned works fall under the ownership of the commissioner.
  • Duration: Copyright has a limited duration depending on a work's category. Ordinarily, a work's copyright lasts 50 years after the death of it's creator.
Types of Licenses

There are many types of licenses a copyright owner can release their content under. Creative Commons licenses are common.

Such as CC BY 4.0, which allows anyone to share, redistribute, and adapt the work. It requires that Attribution is given.

Creative Commons can include more restrictive licenses, such as CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which permits sharing the work but requires Attribution, forbids commercial use, and forbids publishing derivatives.

You can limit your work to "ALL RIGHTS RESERVED", providing no license for anyone to copy, redistribute, or alter your work.

On the opposite end, there is the "UnLicense", which declares your work free for anyone to do anything with. It is popular in the FOSS (free open source software) community.

I would probably license this project under CC BY-NC 4.0, requiring attribution and non-commercial use.

Privacy and Web Privacy Policy
The NZ Privacy Act outlines 13 principles of privacy.
  • 1, Purpose: Only collect personal information if it is necessary.
  • 2, Source: Collect personal data directly from the individual.
  • 3, Notification: Inform individuals why their information is being collected, and who will see it.
  • 4, Manner: Always collect personal information in a fair, lawful, and non-intrusive way.
  • 5, Storage & Security: Secure personal data against loss, misuse, modification, or unauthorised disclosure.
  • 6, Access: Give individuals the right to request access to their data.
  • 7, Correction: Allow individuals to request corrections to their personal data or attach a statement of correction if you decline.
  • 8, Accuracy: Verify that personal information is accurate, up-to-date, complete, and relevant before using it.
  • 9, Retention: Do not keep personal information for any longer than it is required for its lawful purpose.
  • 10, Limits on Use: Only use personal information for the original reason it was collected unless an exception applies.
  • 11, Limits on Disclosure: Do not share personal data with external parties unless authorised, necessary, or covered by a legal exception.
  • 12, Cross-border Disclosure: Only send personal information overseas if the recipient country provides comparable privacy protections.
  • 13, Unique Identifiers: Only assign unique identifiers (like account numbers or IDs) when necessary, and do not reuse identifiers created by other agencies.
How to Improve web SEO
SEO

...or Search Engine Optimization is improving your website so that it ranks better on search engines like google, making people more likely to find it.

Keywords: Search engines use Keywords to matches to a user's search, so knowing and understanding what keywords are likely to be searched by your target audience is vital.

On Page: Google and similar user automated bots that scan every webpage they find to classify it in their databases. Making your website more interpretable by those bots with tags, meta descriptors, and good structure can improve your SEO.

Technical: Google's internal system has many metrics that they use to rank web pages. A site that lodas quickly scores higher, so optimize content, images, and code structure. A sitemap document can help the bots identify all pages to scan.

Off Page: Google also rates your web pages by associations with other pages in their databases. If your web page is linked to often on known and reputable websites, you score higher with these "votes of confidence".

What to consider when choosing a webhosting provider
Where you publish and host your website is important for your success.
Your web host must be:
  • Reliable: The industry standard for uptime is 99.9%, and frequent outages can impact your userbase significantly.
  • Performant: Even a few seconds of loading can lose customers, so a quick loading time is essential.
  • Secure: All good modern webhosts have built in security features such as a WAF Web Application Firewall, protection against common attacks like DDoS, and frequent backups.
  • Supported: Technical issues will happen, and often at inconvenient times. Your host should have customer support available at all times, or at least during your peak hours.
  • Scalable: Your website is likely to grow over time, in both content and users. You should look for web hosts that offer scalability at good costs.

This web project will be hosted on Github Pages, as it meets almost all of the above criteria, in addition to being free. Github Pages is not very scalable, but this is a student project that is not intended to grow significantly, so I do not need to worry about that.

Web Performance and Maintenance
Webpage performance is very important for a number of reasons. But how do you do it?
  • Images: Optimize your images by compressing them. You rarely need images of high resoultions.
  • Caching: Store personalization, and static files such as CSS sheets on a user's local browser so that they dont need to download the data every time they visit.
  • Minify: Minifying removes whitespace, linebreaks, and comments from your code. On a large project this small optimization could save many miliseconds of load time.
Maintenance is also important to keep things running smoothly.
  • Do regular backups: At least weekly backups of your data, web files, and codebase. Make copies off-site so that you can always recover, even after natural disasters.
  • Keep things up to date: Outdated code can contain vulnerabilities that hackers target. Update any libraries. plugins, etc that you use regularly.
  • Monitor: Keep an eye on your servers and database. Computers accumulate junk data while operating that can slow them down.
  • Clean: When server and DB performance drops, clean out all of that junk data.
Web Security
Web security:

Malicious code and hackers are all over the world, and you must make sure your website is not vulnerable.

Common cyberattacks inclide:

  • Phishing attacks: targeting human users and administrators of your website and business. They use social engineering to trick people into opening security holes.
  • Use secure authentication like Multi-Factor Auth and train all business personel to be aware of these threats.
  • Ransomware: Malicious software that encrypts your files and data. This is often a payload after a hacker enters your system through a vulnerability.
  • Maintain automated backups on disconnected systems that can be accessed even if your main system is compromised. Keep all security programs and code up to date.
  • SQL Injection: Attackers can insert malicious SQL code into vulnerable input fields in your website, causing your database to execute unauthorized commands.
  • Sanatize and validate all input fields to prevent SQL injection.
  • Distrubuted Denial of Service (DDoS): Attackers flood your network with a high volume of fake traffic, causing your system to become overwhelmed and crash.
  • A good webhost should include DDoS-protection systems, like Cloudflare. Add rate-limiting systems to your website to block clients that send suspiciously high volumes of traffic.
